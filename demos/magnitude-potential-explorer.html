What the ratio measures

Each point gets a potential from the magnitude weights of its own class and of the whole dataset. A ratio near 1 marks a typical class member. A ratio near 0 marks a point that sits in a dense region of the data but is sparse within its own class, such as boundary, overlapping or mislabelled points.

What t does

Similarities decay as e−t·d. At small t every point sees most of the dataset, so the ratio reflects coarse class layout. At large t each point sees only its nearest neighbours, so the ratio picks out local outliers and the potential field shrinks to small islands around the data.

How it is computed

The page factors S + 10⁻⁵I (Cholesky, float64) for the full set and for each class. Leave-one-out potential at a training point uses the identity p₋ᵢ(xᵢ) = 1 − wᵢ / (S⁻¹)ᵢᵢ, which matches the reference implementation without refitting n times.

Data: make_gaussians(k=3, d=2, n_per_class=500, spread=2, std=1, seed=0), 70% stratified training split. From “Generalization in Neural Networks Through the Lens of Magnitude Potential”, NeurIPS 2026.
